## Spoken script

In this notebook, I use a simulated population of reaction times to understand statistical uncertainty. The population mean is visible, but my sample contains only forty people. Its average misses the truth, which is normal sampling variation.

I then repeat the sampling process to explain the Central Limit Theorem and standard error. Confidence intervals describe how a procedure performs across repeated studies. A p-value instead asks how unusual the observed result would be under a specified null model. Neither number tells me how practically important an effect is.

The group-comparison example shows why pairing matters: analyzing each person's change removes much of the variation between people.

Finally, I apply these ideas to machine learning. Training accuracy, test accuracy, and cross-validation scores differ because they estimate performance with limited data. I compare models on the same examples and demonstrate the winner's curse. Selecting on validation and reporting a separate test score gives a more honest result.
